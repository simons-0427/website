Nutrition calculator

Daily targets, food prices and nutrition values in one model. The calculator searches for the least expensive plan that stays within the chosen limits. It runs locally in the browser.

Data analysis Tool v1.3 6 December 2026

About the project

Why this tool exists

I am heading into a year when money will be tight as a student. At the same time, I do not want to get by on noodles and toast. Eating well on a budget sounds simple. In practice, it means juggling nutrients, prices and portion sizes in your head.

Lists and meal-prep tips were not enough for me. I wanted to know which amounts of which foods cover a daily target and what that costs per day. So I turned the problem into data and an optimisation model instead of relying on guesswork.

Stack: HTML, CSS, JavaScript | Data: JSON | Calculations in the browser, no account required

Features

What the calculator can do

  • Food database: Prices and nutrition values per 100 g, 100 ml or piece. Search, filter, edit and save locally in the browser.
  • Profile: Body data, activity, goal and dietary style shape the daily target and the food pool used for the plan.
  • Requirements: Minimum and maximum values for each nutrient. Profile suggestions can be overridden manually.
  • Optimised plan: A solver finds the least expensive combination that satisfies every limit.
  • Dietary style: Filters for vegan, vegetarian, pescatarian and more.
  • Food properties: Filters for whole food, lactose-free, gluten-free, processed, supplement and more.
  • Contaminants: The optimal plan can exclude foods with defined contaminants.
  • Fluids: The calculator also accounts for fluid requirements.
  • Protein quality: The calculator derives an amino-acid score.
  • Private by default: No server login. Changes stay on the device.

Technical details

How the calculation works

The question is simple: which amounts of which foods cover my daily target at the lowest possible cost? The tool solves it in five steps.

1. Every food has numbers

Each database entry contains a price and nutrition values such as calories, protein and iron. Everything is expressed per 100 g, 100 ml or one piece. The calculator later multiplies amount × nutrient value and amount × price. If the reference unit is wrong, the whole plan is wrong.

2. Your daily target becomes a set of limits

The profile provides suggestions such as “at least this much protein, at most this many calories”. These are constraints: a lower and/or upper limit for each nutrient. You can adjust them. An empty field means that limit is not applied.

3. The food pool gets smaller

Only foods that match your dietary style and filters, such as vegan or lactose-free, enter the calculation. Everything else is removed. Fewer candidates mean less computation, and the plan fits your kitchen better.

4. The solver finds the cheapest mix

For every food in the pool, the solver asks: “How much of it per day?” The total cost should be as small as possible. At the same time, the nutrients from all selected amounts must satisfy every limit.

In formulas, that looks like:

Goal: spend as little money as possible
  = Price₁×Amount₁ + Price₂×Amount₂ + …

For every nutrient:
  Lower bound ≤ sum of all foods ≤ upper bound

Amounts are ≥ 0
(optional: whole grams / pieces only)

Mathematically, this is a linear programme: linear costs, linear nutrient totals and fixed limits. The solver runs in the browser, often in a worker so the page stays responsive, and searches for a feasible solution at minimum cost. If there is none, it reports which limits block the plan.

5. What you get back

A list of foods, amounts and their share of the cost, plus the daily nutrient totals. The limiting factor, often shown with a blue line in the UI, is the nutrient putting the most pressure on the plan: barely reaching its lower limit or sitting close to its upper limit. That is where it is worth checking the database or constraints.

In short: price and nutrition table → profile limits → filters → “the cheapest amounts that satisfy every limit” → plan and the constraint that is binding.

The app is a static page under /en/nutrition/ (HTML/JS with JSON data). Changes are stored locally by the browser. No server performs the calculations.
