Position sizing & risk

How much should you stake
when you have an edge?

Having a real edge is not enough. Position size determines whether that edge compounds over time or whether you go broke on the way.

Simon Stark|Published 16 June 2026|Approx. 6 min read
01

The problem

Too little or too much

In a trade, bet or investment with positive expected value, the size of the position matters. Too little leaves potential growth unused. Too much means one bad outcome can reduce capital so far that recovery becomes impossible, even when the edge is real in the long run.

Core idea: The right size is a risk decision as well as a return decision.
02

Common approaches

Why fixed stakes are usually suboptimal

A fixed euro amount is simple but ignores the size of the edge. It is too small for a particularly strong opportunity and too large for a marginal one. The opposite extreme, putting everything into the opportunity that looks best, is more dangerous: one bad path can wipe out the capital.

03

The mathematical middle ground

Tie position size to the edge

The useful position size rises with win probability and payout ratio. The Kelly criterion calculates that relationship. In practice, many people use only a fraction, such as half Kelly, to reduce short-term volatility at a smaller cost in long-term growth.

For the formula and a full example, see The Kelly criterion explained simply.

04

In practice

Three questions before every opportunity

  1. What is my realistic probability of winning?
  2. What is the payout ratio?
  3. How much capital am I willing to let fluctuate in the short term?

With these values you can calculate an orientation for position size. The number should still be checked against liquidity and the total portfolio.

Worked example

Full versus half Kelly

With a 55% win probability and a payout ratio of 1.2, Kelly gives 22.5% of capital. For €10,000, that is €2,250. Half Kelly reduces it to €1,125.

Full Kelly: €10,000 × 22.5% = €2,250 Half Kelly: €10,000 × 11.25% = €1,125The smaller stake accepts that the estimate may be wrong and that a short losing run should not dictate the whole plan.

Losing streak

An edge does not prevent consecutive losses

Several losses can occur even with positive expected value. If you risk the same percentage of current capital, the euro amount falls automatically. With fixed euro stakes, the next stake becomes larger relative to what remains.

A 20% loss requires a 25% gain to recover; a 50% loss requires 100%. Position size determines not only growth but whether the plan survives. A strategy that works only if results immediately turn good is too aggressive.

Before the stake

Four limits belong before the formula

  • Available capital: Use only money you can genuinely risk.
  • Maximum single loss: Decide in advance what one outcome may cost.
  • Total risk: Check whether several positions depend on the same market, event or factor.
  • Liquidity: Rent, taxes, reserves and emergencies stay outside risk capital.

Use Kelly or a fraction as an orientation afterwards. The best number is not the largest mathematical number, but the one you can follow after a bad month.
